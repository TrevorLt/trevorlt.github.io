CryptScan

From distance measurements to 3D geometry.

A mechatronics prototype built around a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 and a VL53L1X time-of-flight distance sensor, designed to reconstruct the approximate geometry of small objects through controlled rotation and layered measurements.

Focus
Mechatronics & sensing
Stage
In development
Platform
Raspberry Pi Pico 2
Inside the project

The planned motion system pairs a StepperOnline 17HE08-1004S NEMA 17 stepper motor with an HR4988SQ-based driver board to rotate the object. A manually adjustable sensor carriage provides measurements at multiple heights.

I’m developing CryptScan through DSGN 297. The current work focuses on VL53L1X sensor integration, CircuitPython distance acquisition, and USB serial output to a computer. The planned reconstruction pipeline combines distance, commanded rotation angle, and sensor height into 3D coordinates for point-cloud visualization and PLY export.

The prototype targets coarse, recognizable geometry. Motor integration and multilayer reconstruction remain in development; a complete 3D scan has not yet been demonstrated.

About me

I like figuring things out by building them.

I’m Trevor, a computer engineering student at Northwestern University interested in mechatronics and robotics. I’m drawn to the point where mechanical design, electronics, and software come together—and to figuring out how they work as a physical system.

I learn best with something in my hands: a part to model, a wire to solder, or a mechanism to assemble and troubleshoot. My projects have taken me from building combat robots and assembling and tuning FPV drones to developing a distance-sensor-based 3D scanner. I enjoy making a first version work, seeing where it falls short, and trying again. That’s how I’m exploring robotics, one build at a time.
